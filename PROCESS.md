# Process overview
 

## What I built

I built an interactive explainer about how we perceive colour. I initially approached the project broadly as an exploration of colour theory, partly because art and painting are hobbies of mine and I have found colour theory useful when learning to paint and draw. However, I narrowed the project around one stronger idea: what we perceive as colour is influenced by its context and lighting. The final prototype explores this idea through interactive experiments involving simultaneous contrast, coloured lighting, perception and the RGB components of a screen. Rather than presenting colour theory as a collection of facts, I wanted users to discover the concepts by interacting with them and seeing that their perception can differ from the underlying values.

## Moments
**Moment 1 - Finding the visual direction**

One moment that significantly changed the prototype was improving its visual aesthetic. The initial version generated by Claude was very plain and did not reflect the creative nature of the topic. Instead of continuing to add features, I explored different visual directions and chose a Bauhaus-inspired aesthetic with geometric shapes, typography and colour. I then directed Claude to apply this visual language consistently. I knew this was an improvement because the interface became connected to the subject itself, using colour and composition as part of the explanation.

**Moment 2 - Deliberately cutting colour mixing**

A second important moment was deciding not to include an interactive colour-mixing section. I initially considered it because it seemed like a natural part of colour theory, but realised it answered a different question from the rest of the prototype. Mixing explains how a colour stimulus is produced, whereas my central idea concerns how an existing stimulus is perceived in context. Instead of adding it simply to make the prototype longer, I cut it and recorded the decision in CLAUDE.md. This kept the prototype focused on one strong idea and avoided a technically risky simulation that could weaken the explanation.
[9f6ecd1](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass1-jaz0502/commit/9f6ecd1) 

**Moment 3 - Turning experiments into an explainer**

A third moment was improving the website into more of an explainer. After creating the basic design and layout, it was visually engaging but initially felt like a series of experiments rather than something that explained why the effects occurred. Instead of simply adding more descriptive text, I directed Claude to integrate explanations into the interactions through clearer labels, transitions and explanations after each experiment. This shifted the website from demonstrating colour illusions to teaching the central idea that colour perception is influenced by context, light and the visual system.
 [dc08bc8](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass1-jaz0502/commit/dc08bc8)

**Moment 4 - Verifying the pixels illusion**

A fourth moment occurred while developing the Pixels section. The goal was to show a colour that appears purple at normal scale but is revealed as red and blue subpixels when zoomed in. Claude initially used red and green squares, but the result did not appear purple as intended. Instead of accepting the implementation because the code worked, I checked the rendered result and manually directed Claude towards the appropriate red and blue values. I then verified the interaction at both normal and zoomed scales. This reinforced the importance of treating the rendered result as the ground truth when directing an agent to create a visually-driven prototype.
