# Assignment 1 reflection

1. What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

I initially brainstormed ideas based on my hobbies and interests, including tennis, piano, music and art. I became most interested in colour theory because I had been exploring colour while painting, but my early ideas risked becoming a collection of broad colour concepts. The breakthrough was realising that I did not need to explain “colour theory” as a whole, but could instead build the prototype around one central question: “Is colour really what is there?” Narrowing the project to the idea that colour is influenced by context, light and perception gave me a much clearer direction and one strong idea to build around. This also helped me make deliberate scope decisions. For example, I considered including colour mixing, afterimages and other colour-model concepts, but cut ideas that introduced separate mechanisms rather than strengthening the central idea. The resulting prototype became a sequence of interactive experiments where users can discover for themselves how surrounding factors influence what they perceive as colour.

2. What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

This work made me more interested in creating software that is interactive, visual and engaging rather than primarily focused on presenting information. I particularly enjoyed using interaction to make users discover an idea for themselves instead of simply explaining it to them. I also learned that a smaller idea with a clear point of view can be more effective than adding features simply to make a project feel bigger. Going forward, I want to become a developer who thinks carefully about the experience I am creating for the user, not just whether the underlying software works.
